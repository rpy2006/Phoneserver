Privacy policy

PhoneServer is a local-network service with no backend. This policy explains exactly what the app and these static pages process, where it is stored, and — the short version — what never leaves the device.

1. Scope and who we are

This policy covers PhoneServer 1.0 (package com.yadaventerprise.phoneserver), a self-hosted file server, website host and local data platform for Android, together with the static documentation, guide and legal pages served next to it. It is published by Yadav Enterprise as the project's publisher — see the legal notice for full identification and third-party attributions.

PhoneServer is deliberately local-first: it runs on your device, on your network, with no account system, no cloud service and no telemetry upload. There is no "we may share your data with our partners" section below, because there is no pipeline to share it through.

2. What PhoneServer processes

All processing happens on the device you installed the app on, and only when you use it.

DataWhyWhere it lives
Files you browse, upload or download Core file-server features; media is scanned so Gallery/Music see it Your shared storage (Pictures/, Download/, …) or the app's private folder if All files access is off
Access password and username Optional gate for /files, uploads and remote browsing App-private SharedPreferences on the device
Saved remote server profiles Connect to other phones running PhoneServer App-private preferences on the device
Request counters, bytes, client IPs and user agents Live dashboard counters and the recent-activity feed In memory while the server runs; not written to disk by the app
Projects, databases and files you create Local SQL and object-storage platform under /api/v1 App-private data root on the device
Chat messages and imported .gguf models On-device AI chat — inference runs locally App-private storage; nothing is sent to a model provider
Backup archives you export Restore or move data between devices Wherever you choose to save the file

Known storage caveat. The optional access password is stored in SharedPreferences without encryption — this is documented as a known limitation in the security model. Treat the device itself as trusted, and see the hardening checklist in the production guide.

3. Android permissions

  • Internet / network state / Wi-Fi state — run the local server, discover your LAN address, reach other phones.
  • All files access (optional) — serve and upload into the real shared folders. Without it, everything falls back to the app's private sandbox.
  • Foreground service and notifications — keep the server alive with an ongoing status notification.
  • Wake lock — hold a partial wake lock while serving so the screen can lock without dropping connections.
  • Boot completed (optional) — auto-start the server after a reboot if you enable it.
  • Camera (optional) — scan a QR code when pairing another phone.

Every permission is used only for the feature above. None of them feeds an analytics or advertising SDK — there are none in the build.

4. Where data is stored

  • App-private storage — preferences, project databases, the served website, imported models, backups metadata. Uninstalling the app removes it.
  • Shared storage — only what you explicitly upload or create through the file server, in normal Android media/download folders.
  • Backups — zip archives you export yourself; they contain your databases, files and a metadata.json. Store and delete them like any other personal file.

5. What leaves the device

PhoneServer speaks plain HTTP on port 8080 and is designed for a trusted local network. Within that boundary, other devices on your LAN can reach the server — and therefore the data you chose to share through it, protected only by the password you set.

  • No internet transfer. The app does not upload usage data, crash reports, identifiers or content to any server we operate or to anyone else.
  • No accounts, no identifiers. There is no registration, no advertising ID use and no cross-app tracking.
  • Your responsibility at the edge. If you port-forward or otherwise expose port 8080 to the public internet, you are extending the trust boundary yourself. The project explicitly does not support that deployment — there is no TLS.

6. Analytics and tracking

We do not use analytics, crash-reporting, attribution or advertising tools, in the app or on these pages. The dashboard numbers you see are measured by the local server for your own use; they are never transmitted to us, because there is no receiver.

7. This documentation site

The guide, documentation and legal pages are plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. They load no external scripts, no web fonts from a CDN, no embedded videos and no third-party widgets. They set no cookies of their own; preference values (documentation theme, cookie consent) are stored in your browser's local storage on this device only. Full detail is in the cookies policy.

If you serve these pages through PhoneServer itself, requests to them appear like any other local request in the in-memory dashboard counters described above.

8. Third parties

PhoneServer contains no third-party SDKs for analytics, ads or marketing. Open-source libraries bundled for functionality only (AndroidX, Material Components, ZXing, llama.cpp) are listed with their licences in the legal notice. They do not collect or transmit personal data on our behalf.

9. Children

PhoneServer is a general-purpose tool and is not directed at children under 13. Since nothing is collected, we do not knowingly process children's data — but because the server exposes files on your LAN, we recommend not sharing access with devices or people you would not hand your phone to.

10. Retention and deletion

  • Delete content in the app — removed from storage like any other file operation.
  • Clear the data / uninstall — removes preferences, passwords, profiles, projects and databases.
  • In-memory counters — discarded when the server stops.
  • Browser storage on these pages — delete it in your browser settings (site data / local storage) to reset the theme and the cookie choice.

11. Your rights

Because your data stays on your device, the practical controls are access, correction and deletion through the app itself, plus uninstall at any time. Where applicable law gives you rights over personal data (for example access, rectification, erasure or objection), you can exercise them by contacting us using the channel you received PhoneServer from — we hold no copy of your data to act on, but we will confirm and explain.

12. Changes and contact

If the app's data behaviour changes, this page will be updated with a new version number and effective date, and the revised text will ship with the release and the documentation. Continued use after a change means you accept the updated policy for that version.

Questions about this policy: use the repository issue tracker or the distribution channel you obtained PhoneServer from. For how terms govern your use, see the Terms of Service.
